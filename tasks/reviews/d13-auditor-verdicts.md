## 2. Per-row evidence

### 1. cvx-2015 Item 2 — WRONG (confidence 0.95 and review_required False)

Span `[107079,107613)` opens at `Item 2. Properties` and closes exactly before `Item 3. Legal Proceedings` at 107613 — verified by printing `normalized_text[107030:107700]`. The boundary is right.

The body is nothing but three cross-references. Every target is present in this same `normalized_text` and attributed to no item:

- "described on page 3 under Item 1" → Item 1's span `[9725,92632)`.
- "Tables I through VII on pages FS-61 through FS-71" → `Table I` occurrences at 366212, 369942, 373443.
- "Note 16, 'Properties, Plant and Equipment,' … is on page FS-41" → `Properties, Plant and Equipment` at 191859 / 193839.

Offsets 366212, 191859 and 193839 all lie in `[123226, 417517)` — the 294,291-character tail after Item 15's span, which carries no item attribution at all (this is what drives `coverage=0.2718`).

The output publishes `confidence=0.95`, the top of the scale, and `review_required=False`. A consumer thresholding at 0.9 accepts 534 characters of pointer as Chevron's properties disclosure with no flag. The contract says confidence "must be honest: downstream consumers will threshold on it." A value that assigns 0.95 to a pure cross-reference cannot discriminate the thing consumers threshold for.

### 2. cvx-2015 Item 6 — WRONG (confidence 0.95 and review_required False)

Span `[112301,112420)` = 119 chars, pure pointer to "page FS-60". The target — `Five-Year Financial Summary` — is at offset 363489, in the unattributed tail.

The decisive evidence is internal to this one filing: **Item 6 is 119 characters and is published at `0.95 / review_required=False`, while Item 8 on the same page is 189 characters — longer — and published at `0.8 / review_required=True`.** Both are pure pointers into the same FS section. The shorter, equally contentless item is published as the *more* trustworthy of the two. Whatever the signal is meant to mean, it is inverted here.

My corpus sweep explains why and generalises it: across all 45 fixtures plus the held-out set, `item_span_near_empty` is emitted for exactly three item codes — `{'7': 7, '8': 15, '1': 2}`. Items 2, 3, 5, 6, 7A, 9, 9B can never receive it however empty they are. Since the contract defines `review_required` as "true exactly when some warning in `warnings` carries this item's code", `review_required` is structurally unable to fire on a near-empty Item 2, 3, 5, 6 or 7A. That is a discriminator that cannot discriminate.

## 3. The shared structure

All nine are one shape: **the item's entire body is a cross-reference, and the published `status` is `extracted`.** Underneath that, two distinct defects:

**Structure A — target outside the selected document (1 item: mrk-1995 Item 5).** The referenced content is in a sibling EDGAR `<DOCUMENT>` (EX-13). This is textbook incorporation by reference, the pipeline recognises this exact shape everywhere else in the corpus, and it recognises it on Item 6 of this very filing. Unambiguous defect, no spec reading required.

**Structure B — target inside the same document, unattributed or attributed to another item (8 items).** Split by where the target ended up:

- *Attributed to no item at all:* cvx 2, 6, 7A, 8 (targets at 191859, 363489, 176669, 210065, all in the unattributed 294k tail); spatz 8 (target at 48815, in the unattributed 17k tail).
- *Attributed to a different item:* bac 3 (target at 549832, inside Item 8); bac 6 (targets at 84778 / 367261, inside Item 7); jpm 7 (target at 223093, inside Item 15).

Structure B is where the spec is genuinely ambiguous. The contract's IBR clause says the span points at "the sentence naming **the other document**", which reads as external-only; under that reading `extracted` is the value the rule produces for all eight. But four of the eight say "incorporated herein by reference" or "included in this report" in their own text, and no consumer reading `status` can tell a complete Item 3 from a pointer Item 3. Per my charter this is a spec ambiguity that must be settled by an ADR, not by argument — I am giving the reading I hold, not claiming it is already written down.

The structure does not change any verdict, but it changes the *reason* for six of them. For cvx 2/6/7A, bac 3/6 and mrk 5 the verdict stands on `confidence`/`review_required` alone, independent of the status question: six items published at 0.95 with `review_required=False`, none of which contains the disclosure it claims, five of them on envelopes that give the consumer no other warning. That part is a calibration finding, not a definitional one, and it survives whichever way the ADR goes.

The second structural finding is mechanical and independent of any reading: **`item_span_near_empty` fires for exactly three item codes corpus-wide — 1, 7, 8** (`{'7': 7, '8': 15, '1': 2}` across 45 fixtures plus held-out). Because `review_required` is defined as "a warning carries this item's code", `review_required` is structurally incapable of firing on a near-empty Item 2, 3, 5, 6, 7A or 9. cvx Item 6 at 119 chars scoring `0.95/False` against cvx Item 8 at 189 chars scoring `0.8/True` is that fact in a single filing.

## 4. What I hold weakly

- **cvx-2015 Item 8, spatz-2014 Item 8, jpm-2024 Item 7** are my three weakest WRONGs. All three signal honestly (0.8/0.75, `review_required=True`, and jpm additionally `doc_status=ambiguous`). Their defect is purely the Structure-B definitional question. If an ADR rules that an intra-document pointer stays `extracted` and that item spans are heading-to-heading with no obligation to resolve internal references, I would concede all three to RIGHT. I would **not** concede rows 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 (the 0.95/`False` ones) or row 9 on that ruling.
- **cvx-2015 Item 2** is the weakest of the 0.95 group, because its main target — "page 3 under Item 1" — *is* correctly extracted, under Item 1. A consumer holding the whole envelope does have Chevron's properties disclosure. It stays WRONG for me because a per-item consumer gets 534 chars at top-of-scale confidence with no flag, and because the Subpart 1200 tables it also names (offset 366212+) are attributed nowhere.
- **I could not determine the mechanism** behind the mrk-1995 Item 5/6 divergence. My heading-wrap hypothesis fails on Item 8. The behaviour is reproducible; the cause is not established.
- **`COVERAGE_MIN`.** I bounded it only behaviourally to (0.0303, 0.2718]. I have no view on whether 0.2718 *should* escalate, only that it currently does not and that the contract's wording ("implausibly low") invites the question.
