## The number, and what it is worth

**4/6, and both failures were mine.** Neither red was an extractor defect. Both
were length floors I derived wrong, in the same way, on the last item of two
different filings in the same sitting.

That makes the honest headline **not** "4/6" and **not** "6/6" either. It is:

> Three fresh, never-observed filings — a 1996 pharmaceutical text submission
> with no contents page, a crisis-era bank that omits four item headings
> outright, and a 9.8 MB REIT — were extracted with every item, status, label
> and boundary correct on the first attempt. The two pre-registered predictions
> that were about the *extractor* held. The one that was about a *document* was
> wrong, and it was wrong because I measured a gap instead of reading a filing.

The other three cases (`csco-2016`, `cost-2022`, `pgr-2023`) are repeats. They
measure regression across the four T9 tranches, which is worth having — ADR-015
and ADR-017 both moved `classify`, and neither disturbed them — but they are not
generalization evidence and are not counted as such here.

## The two reds

### `spg-2019` item 16: floor 20,000, actual 18,351

Simon Property Group's Item 16 body is the single word **"None."** The ~55,700
chars I measured for it were the **exhibit index that follows**, which the
segmentation model annexes to the preceding item — exactly as it annexes WMT's
Executive Officers chart under ADR-014 §1, and correctly.

The frozen prediction read *"Item 16 is PRESENT and substantive"*. The first
half held and is still the first observation anywhere in either set of Item 16's
non-`omitted` branch. The second half was a reading of a gap measurement, not of
the filing.

### `axp-2008` item 15: floor 5,000, actual 745

American Express lists neither its financial statements nor its exhibits inline,
pointing to "page F-1 hereof" and "pages E-1 through E-4 hereof under Exhibit
Index". 745 chars is the whole item. The 19,895 I floored against was the gap
from the heading to the end of the scan text — exhibit index plus signature
block, which the extractor correctly cuts at `TAIL_RE`.

### The systematic mistake

**A length floor derived from the gap to the next heading is meaningless for the
last item**, because that gap runs to the end of the file and sweeps in material
the extractor is right to exclude. I made it twice, on the last item of two
filings, in one sitting.

This is the **fifth** time the verification instrument rather than the pipeline
has been at fault (H1's `xom-2021` Item 9C, H2's `pgr-2023` floating comma, a
declined `csco-2016` assertion, T9's `wfc-2008` heading shape, now this) — and
the second in this milestone. The pattern is no longer a coincidence worth
noting once per triage; the scan is reliable for *what a document contains* and
unreliable for *how much of it belongs to what*, and the length-floor idea this
refresh introduced sits squarely on the unreliable side.

Both floors are corrected from the documents and replaced with cross-item
exclusions, which assert the boundary directly instead of proxying for it
through a length. On the H1 precedent the corrected assertions now measure the
correction rather than the extractor on any future run, and that is recorded in
both cases' provenance.

## What held

- **`mrk-1995` green on the first attempt.** Fresh, never observed: a 1996
  plain-text full submission with **no table of contents at all**, the 14-code
  pre-Item-7A taxonomy, and a legacy Part IV Item 14. It carries length floors
  and cross-item exclusions, so this is the **first held-out pass in the
  project's history that says anything about boundaries** rather than only about
  presence and status — the gap the pre-B audit called finding 4 and H2's triage
  repeated.
- **`axp-2008`'s real subject, and its frozen threshold prediction, held
  exactly.** The strings "Item 10" through "Item 13" occur zero times in that
  filing; all four reported `missing` — the INV-S4 answer, not `omitted`. Four
  of twenty is 0.20, `expected_items_mostly_missing` did not fire, and the run
  reported `success_with_warning`. A document that drops an entire Part passes as
  a qualified success, four percentage points under the line ADR-013 measured.
  **That number was not tuned against this filing and must not be**; it is T10's
  to calibrate, and this is now the closest real filing to it.
- **`spg-2019`'s second prediction held.** Its Item 7A heading reads "Qualitative
  and Quantitative Disclosures About Market Risk" — the filer transposed the
  first two words — and the item resolved normally, as predicted, because title
  similarity is a character ratio. 9.8 MB, `doc_status: success`, no warnings.

## What this run does NOT show

- **No evidence for ADR-017.** The obvious claim to make is that the pointer
  ruling written for Wells Fargo generalised to American Express, which also
  reports items 6, 7, 7A, 8 and 14 as `incorporated_by_reference`. It did not:
  AXP writes single-sentence "…is incorporated herein by reference", which the
  *old* rule already matched. Checked before writing this, and the claim
  withdrawn. ADR-017 remains supported by exactly one filing.
- **Nothing about the span-coverage gap.** ADR-015 §5 records that nothing in
  the battery measures coverage, and this run could not have caught it: all six
  filings resolve cleanly. `axp-2008` comes closest, and passes.
- **Nothing about thresholds.** Every floor and band in the battery is still
  pinned from one side only (ADR-016 consequences).
