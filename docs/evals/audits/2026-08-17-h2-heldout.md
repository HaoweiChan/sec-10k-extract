## The two predictions, resolved

Recorded in `pgr-2023-heldout.json` before the first run, so they could not be
retrofitted. One held. One was wrong, and instructively so.

### Prediction 1 — the Item 1C era boundary: HELD, and the table was right

Period end 2023-12-31 sits 16 days past `ADDED["1C"] = 2023-12-15`. Item 1C
came back `extracted`, 23 items, `doc_status: success`, zero warnings. The era
table has been wrong twice before (Item 9C keyed to period end instead of
filing date; Item 15 on the 2002–2003 transitional window, still enumerated
debt) — at its narrowest available margin, on a filing nothing was tuned
against, it was right.

### Prediction 2 — the floating comma: WRONG, and it is my instrument's fault again

I predicted the cover's `December 31 , 2023` might defeat `COVER_DATE_RE` and
collapse the taxonomy. `period_end` resolved to 2023-12-31, `taxonomy_era`
modern, all 23 items present.

The prediction was not merely lucky-wrong — **the premise was an artifact of my
own verification scan**, for the third time:

```
RAW            …fiscal year ended <ix:nonNumeric …>…December&#160;31</…>, 2023…
MY CRUDE SCAN  'fiscal year ended December 31 , 2023'      <- tags → spaces
THE NORMALIZER 'fiscal year ended December 31, 2023'       <- joins within block
```

There is no floating comma in the text the pipeline reads. My scan replaces
tags with spaces and therefore *manufactures* the defect it then predicts.

This is the same instrument error H1 caught on `xom-2021` (asserting Item 9C
absent when it was present) and that I explicitly avoided on `csco-2016` (by
declining to assert Item 16's absence). I applied the lesson to **absences**
and then made the identical mistake in the shape of a **prediction**. The rule
generalises further than I wrote it down: *any* claim derived from the
tag-strip scan about character-level detail — presence, absence, or
punctuation — is unsound, because the scan and the normalizer disagree about
exactly that.

The floating-comma debt ADR-010 records is still real and still unfixed; it
simply lives on a path this filing never took. `normalize.period_end` tag-strips
the dei window itself, and the lenient `DATE_RE` handles the float there. The
debt bites only where `COVER_DATE_RE` is the sole gate — a filing with no SGML
header, no dei fact, and a genuinely floating comma in normalized text. No
fixture exercises that, and this run did not either.
